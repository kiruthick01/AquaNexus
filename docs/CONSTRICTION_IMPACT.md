# The four flagged cross-sections

Generated by `scripts/constriction_impact.py`, against the model as it was
*before* this change — that is the comparison the decision rested on. The
sections were removed as a result, both models were retrained on the remaining
49, and every number elsewhere in the project reflects that.

The validator flags four sections as constrictions: the channel is far narrower than at either neighbour, because the centreline wanders off the channel and the cut catches a slice of bank. They are artefacts of the extraction, not features of the river.

| section | width | neighbours |
|---|---|---|
| RS 12500 | 37 m | 138 m / 113 m |
| RS 14000 | 11 m | 155 m / 34 m |
| RS 18000 | 14 m | 80 m / 50 m |
| RS 24000 | 14 m | 136 m / 126 m |

## Reach means at 8.1 m3/s

| geometry | depth (m) | velocity (m/s) | top width (m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 53 | 2.650 | 0.371 | 61.2 |
| constrictions dropped | 2.667 | 0.355 | 60.3 |

## What dropping them does to the predictions

Every observation re-predicted with each geometry's hydraulics, chemistry and date held fixed.

| geometry | mean predicted DO (mg/L) | mean shift | worst shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 53 | 7.063 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| constrictions dropped | 7.397 | 0.377 | 1.104 |

## Verdict

Dropping the four sections moves predictions by 0.377 mg/L on average and 1.104 mg/L at worst - 64% of the model's 1.713 mg/L RMSE.

Excluding them would change published conclusions, so it should be done - and every number in the project restated against the new geometry.

For comparison, the roughness constant is worth up to 64% of the same error (`MANNING_SENSITIVITY.md`). Two independent choices in the geometry pipeline each account for a comparable share of the model's error - which says the limit on this project is the hydraulic model, not the regression on top of it.
